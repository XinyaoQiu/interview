# Loop Onsite — Conversation Practice

These are imagined conversations for practice, not Loop's confirmed office procedures or interview questions. Use the responses that fit the situation. The emphasis is on natural English for arriving, meeting people, moving between interviews, and leaving.

## 1. Arriving at reception or the front desk

**Receptionist:** Hi! How can I help you?

**You:** Hi, I'm Xinyao Qiu. I'm here for an onsite engineering interview with Loop.

**Receptionist:** Who are you meeting with?

**You:** I have a few interviews scheduled today, including one with Shaosu. I can show you the invitation if that helps.

**Receptionist:** Great. Could you sign in here?

**You:** Of course.

**Receptionist:** Someone will come get you in a few minutes. Please have a seat.

**You:** Thank you!

## 2. If you're unsure where to go

**You:** Excuse me, is this the right entrance for Loop? I'm here for an interview.

**Staff member:** Yes, you're in the right place. Have you checked in yet?

**You:** Not yet. Where should I check in?

**Staff member:** Just over there at the front desk.

**You:** Thanks for your help.

## 3. Meeting your recruiter or host

**Host:** Hi, Xinyao? I'm coordinating your interviews today. Nice to meet you!

**You:** Hi! Nice to meet you too. Thanks for having me.

**Host:** Did you have any trouble finding us?

**You:** No, it was pretty straightforward. Thanks for sending the directions.

**If you did have trouble:** I had a little trouble finding the entrance, but I got here fine.

**Host:** How's your day going?

**You:** Good, thanks! I've been looking forward to meeting everyone in person. How's yours?

## 4. If someone asks how to pronounce your name

**Interviewer:** Am I saying your name correctly?

**You:** Let me say it for you: Xinyao. Thanks for asking!

Say your name at your normal pace, then repeat it slowly if needed.

## 5. Being offered water or coffee

**Host:** Can I get you some water or coffee?

**You:** Water would be great, thank you.

**Host:** Still or sparkling?

**You:** Still, please.

**If you already have a drink:** I'm all set, thank you.

## 6. Walking through the office

**Host:** Let me show you around quickly. This is where the engineering team sits.

**You:** Nice! Do the product and engineering teams sit near each other?

**Host:** Yes, we work together pretty closely.

**You:** That makes sense. I'm interested in hearing more about how the teams collaborate.

**Host:** And this is the room we'll use for your interviews.

**You:** Great, thank you.

## 7. Reviewing the schedule

**Host:** You'll have a technical interview, a cross-functional conversation, and a conversation with Shaosu.

**You:** Sounds good. Will I stay in this room, or move between rooms?

**Host:** You'll stay here, and the interviewers will come to you.

**You:** Got it, thanks.

**If you need to check the technical format:** Just to confirm, what format should I expect for the technical interview today?

## 8. Asking for the restroom or a moment to get ready

**You:** Before we get started, could you point me to the restroom?

**Host:** Sure, it's down the hall on the left.

**You:** Thank you. I'll be right back.

**If you need to set up:** Could I have a moment to get my laptop ready?

**If you need internet access:** Is there a guest Wi-Fi network I should use?

## 9. Meeting an interviewer

**Interviewer:** Hi, I'm on the engineering team. Nice to meet you.

**You:** Hi, I'm Xinyao. Nice to meet you too.

**Interviewer:** How has your visit been so far?

**You:** It's been good! It's nice to see the office and meet the team in person.

**Interviewer:** Great. Let's start with a quick introduction.

**You:** Sure. My background is in backend engineering. At NewsBreak, I worked on Premium subscriptions and an upload rate limiter, and I also worked with two other engineers on an AI on-call assistant. Earlier, during my TikTok internship, I worked on a large database cleanup project. I'm excited to learn more about the role and the team today.

## 10. Starting the technical interview

**Interviewer:** I'll describe the problem, and then we can work through it together.

**You:** Sounds good. Is it okay if I take a few notes as you explain it?

**Interviewer:** Of course.

**You:** Thanks. Before I start proposing a solution, I'd like to clarify a couple of requirements.

**If the interviewer asks about a past project:** I'd be happy to walk through the subscription system. I'll start with the goal and overall design, then explain the main tradeoffs.

**If it's a system design problem:** Let me start with the users and the main workflow, and then we can discuss the components and failure cases.

## 11. Asking for clarification

**Interviewer:** How would you handle that at scale?

**You:** When you say scale, are you mainly thinking about request volume, data size, or the number of customers?

**Interviewer:** Let's focus on request volume.

**You:** Got it. In that case, I'd first look at which part of the request path becomes the bottleneck.

**If you didn't hear something:** Sorry, I missed the last part. Could you repeat that?

**If you don't understand a term:** I'm not familiar with that term in this context. Could you explain what it means here?

**If you want to confirm your understanding:** So the main concern is preventing duplicate actions when a request is retried. Is that right?

## 12. Taking time to think or correcting yourself

**Interviewer:** What would happen if those two operations ran at the same time?

**You:** Let me think through that for a moment.

**You, after thinking:** Actually, I see a problem with my earlier approach. Both operations could read the same state before either writes an update. I'd need to handle that conflict explicitly.

**If you don't know the answer:** I haven't worked with that directly, so I don't want to guess. I can explain how I'd investigate it and what I'd want to verify.

**If you need to restart an explanation:** Let me explain that more clearly. The main point is that the update needs to happen only once.

## 13. Meeting the cross-functional interviewer

**Interviewer:** Hi! I work on the product team. This will be a conversation about how you work with other people and approach customer problems.

**You:** Nice to meet you. I'm looking forward to hearing your perspective on how engineering and product work together here.

**Interviewer:** Let's say a customer urgently needs a feature, but building it would delay another project. What would you do?

**You:** I'd start by understanding why it's urgent and what happens if we miss the deadline. Do we know whether the customer is blocked, or whether there's a temporary workaround?

**Interviewer:** Let's assume they're blocked, but the other project also has an important deadline.

**You:** Then I'd compare the impact of both delays and look for a smaller solution that unblocks the customer. I'd bring a recommendation to the product manager and the affected teams, so we can agree on the tradeoff and communicate any timeline changes.

## 14. Meeting Shaosu

**Shaosu:** Hi, Xinyao. I'm Shaosu. Nice to meet you.

**You:** Nice to meet you too. Thanks for taking the time to talk with me.

**Shaosu:** How has your day been so far?

**You:** It's been good. I've enjoyed meeting the team and learning more about how people work together here.

**Shaosu:** What interests you about Loop?

**You:** There are three main reasons. First, I want to work in a fast-paced startup environment where I can take ownership and see the impact of my work. Second, I want to work on AI agents, especially the systems that help them complete real tasks reliably. Third, Loop's work applying AI to logistics is interesting to me. The articles on domain-driven design and engineering growth stood out: engineers learn from domain experts and get responsibility early, with support from teammates. That combination appeals to me.

The company-specific points above come from the [job description](https://ats.rippling.com/loop/jobs/f42fddce-022c-47be-8c57-1b2bb3ee3a7c), [domain-driven design article](https://www.loop.com/engineering-blog/domain-driven-design-at-loop), and [engineering growth article](https://www.loop.com/engineering-blog/how-engineers-grow-at-loop), reviewed during preparation on October 26, 2026.

## 15. Asking questions at the end of an interview

**Interviewer:** What questions do you have for me?

**You, to an engineer:** What has been the most challenging part of the system you've worked on recently?

**You, to a product or design partner:** What makes an engineer especially effective when working with your team?

**You, to Shaosu:** The JD mentions helping agents and non-technical users do work that previously required an engineer. What's one workflow you're especially excited to make possible?

**A natural follow-up:** That's interesting. What's the biggest obstacle to getting there?

Ask one question at a time and respond to the answer before moving to another.

## 16. Moving between interviews

**Interviewer:** That's all the time we have. Thanks for talking with me.

**You:** Thank you! I enjoyed the conversation.

**Host:** How are you doing? Ready for the next one?

**You:** Yes, I'm ready.

**If you need a break:** Could I take a quick restroom break before we start?

**If asked how the last round went:** It was a good discussion. The problem gave me a lot to think about.

## 17. Casual conversation if there's a break or lunch

**Teammate:** Is this your first time visiting our office?

**You:** Yes, it's nice to meet everyone in person. How long have you been at Loop?

**Teammate:** A little over a year.

**You:** What surprised you most when you joined?

**Teammate:** Probably how much there was to learn about logistics.

**You:** I can imagine. What helped you get familiar with the domain?

Other simple questions you can use:

- What are you working on at the moment?
- What do you enjoy most about working here?
- Do people usually get lunch together?

## 18. If an interviewer is running late

**Host:** Sorry, your next interviewer is running a few minutes behind.

**You:** No problem. Thanks for letting me know.

**If you've been waiting without an update:** Hi, I just wanted to check whether I should keep waiting here for the next interview.

## 19. Wrapping up with the host or recruiter

**Host:** That's everything for today. Thanks for coming in!

**You:** Thank you for organizing everything. I enjoyed meeting the team and learning more about the role.

**Host:** We'll be in touch about next steps.

**You:** Great. What timeline should I expect for an update?

**Host:** We should have an update in the next few days.

**You:** Sounds good. Thanks again, and have a great rest of your day!

## 20. Saying goodbye to someone you met earlier

**You:** It was nice meeting you. Thanks again for the conversation!

**Teammate:** You too. Have a safe trip home.

**You:** Thanks! Take care.
